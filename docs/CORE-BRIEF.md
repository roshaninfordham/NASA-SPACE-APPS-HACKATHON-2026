## Experience

2053, Mars, Sol 18. The player tests AEGIS with crewmate Mira on the radio. The playable explorer inspects the solar array; Mira is a narrative companion, not an independently simulated habitat operator. An approaching dust storm and an independent solar particle event force decisions across three connected scales:

1. **Habitat:** deposited dust reduces solar generation; atmospheric dust blocks sunlight independently. Cleaning costs habitat power. Conservation reduces nonessential electrical demand and slows modeled research work. Essential life support cannot be switched off.
2. **Astronaut:** an EVA uses a separate suit battery with automatic life support. Returning takes time. Completing an inspection restores array capacity, so retreating early can sacrifice equipment performance.
3. **Visor:** cleaning improves visibility and inspection progress but consumes suit energy. It does not block ionizing radiation.

Shelter is preinstalled passive shielding. It does not materialize or continuously consume electricity. These mechanisms implement the brief's operational adaptation, rather than an implausible force field.

## Mission and learning

A four-minute session compresses a twelve-hour fictional emergency. The player can pause freely. Protect the crew, maintain critical power, and retain recovery energy. Mistakes end in a gentle mission abort and a useful explanation, not graphic injury. A debrief compares the player's run against a declared fixed strategy in identical weather. No claim that adaptive control always wins.

The core question: **Can you coordinate habitat, astronaut, and visor protection without exhausting the energy needed to survive?**

For ages 7–12, lead with concrete verbs and visible consequences. Put equations and caveats in the field guide. Adult facilitation may be needed for younger readers. Actual comprehension and enjoyment require supervised child playtesting; they have not yet been established.

## AEGIS mission planner

The current source extends that core with a local, deterministic comparison of four fixed policies: normal work or power conservation, each with panel cleaning off or on. During a running mission, **P** or **Pause → Compare mission plans** opens a paused one-simulated-hour preview from the player's current state. It runs the actual mission equations; it does not call a cloud service or learn a policy.

The preview keeps the explorer's shelter state and visor setting unchanged and performs no new inspection. It displays battery, optional research, dose, suit energy, duration and abort status. A taller battery bar is not a safety score, especially if that plan stops early. The learner predicts a trade-off, compares the result and may explicitly choose **Use this plan** to change only conservation and panel cleaning. Returning to shelter remains the player's decision and movement. The source addition is not included in the published 0.6.1 installer; its eventual release requires separate acceptance.

## Boundaries

Water recycling, food, pressure, and thermal control are background systems represented through a baseline electrical demand. They are not independently simulated. AEGIS is a transparent rules-based adviser, not machine learning. This is an educational prototype, not professional astronaut certification, medical guidance, or a validated spacecraft design tool.

## Replaceable presentation

Characters, habitat geometry, colors, audio, and interface treatment are provisional. They must not encode the simulation rules. Replace the scene adapter without changing energy or dose calculations. Change scenario parameters without rewriting the renderer. The owner’s later visual specifications guide the art.
