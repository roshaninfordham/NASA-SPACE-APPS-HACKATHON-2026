# The science behind the mission journey

KŌMAKU: Shield Protocol is a learning game for ages 7–12. The mission connects four useful ideas: **cleaning changes panel transmission; solar power and load change stored battery energy; radiation dose accumulates with outside time**. It uses observations to explain the ideas and explicit scenario settings to make a short, playable activity.

This document describes the final 0.8 pace/fatigue model, superseding the earlier recall/forecast journey. It is separate from retained legacy first-person and dashboard experiments. The child-facing source of truth is [mission-concepts.json](../game/data/mission-concepts.json). The final workflow is reconciled in [FINAL-WORKFLOW.md](design-specs/FINAL-WORKFLOW.md); the earlier JOURNEY-0.8 design is historical. Historical-model sources were checked on November 14, 2026, America/New_York. The SDO live-image integration and source-use policy were checked on November 15, 2026, America/New_York.

The NASA archive record for Hassler et al. is metadata-only. The primary manuscript was checked at Caltech; Table 1 explicitly names the tissue-like E detector and reports **0.21 ± 0.04 mGy/day**, equivalent to **210 ± 40 µGy/day**. The paper distinguishes its approximately 300-sol observation interval from the per-day dose rates. We use the Earth-day rate convention in the numerical model rather than silently changing the denominator to a sol.

NASA's December 2013 news release reports **0.67 mSv/day** for the same broad observation period. The primary manuscript gives **0.64 ± 0.12 mSv/day** with an average radiation quality factor. Both are dose-equivalent figures, not the absorbed-dose input used here. We do not substitute either number for µGy or infer a general conversion factor from their ratio.

## Live NASA solar observation, separate from the mission model

The solar view retrieves NASA SDO’s [latest available 512 px AIA 193 Å image](https://sdo.gsfc.nasa.gov/assets/img/latest/latest_512_0193.jpg), listed on [The Sun Now](https://sdo.gsfc.nasa.gov/data/). This is a colorized extreme-ultraviolet **browse image** from the Atmospheric Imaging Assembly, not a Mars dosimeter or a numerical dose forecast. It provides a live online source for observation; the mission’s historical 210 µGy/day baseline, authored radiation ramp, pace/fatigue, deadline, power model, and outcome rules are independent of the image. No image pixels are converted into physical values.

The client states are **loading, live, stale, cached, and fallback**. “Live” means a valid connection during this app process, not a live video stream or proof of a recent exposure. File publication age over 24 hours is labelled stale even after a successful request; this is an authored interface policy, not a NASA forecast rule. Cache age is measured from successful retrieval, separately from file publication age. Unknown times remain unknown. When no usable cache exists, a bundled NASA example remains explicitly labelled as a bundled fallback.

HTTP `Last-Modified`, if supplied, is server-file metadata, not a verified exposure timestamp. A newly downloaded file can contain an older observation; neither availability nor freshness is guaranteed. Browser CORS behavior needs its own verification; a native HTTPS success does not establish browser fetching. The source-page button offers an external-browser fallback. ## The final model, step by step

The story leads directly to a **preflight control room**. The player may inspect the eight concepts and set a starting pace; concept review is optional and does not gate Start. No work, fatigue, mission time, energy, or dose advances before **START MISSION**. Ren stays at the visible panel beside the shelter door, and the operator chooses **SLOWER / FASTER** or a bounded slider. At 30 simulated minutes Ren automatically enters shelter: no recall, walking distance, or additional entry time.

The authoritative implementation is `game/scripts/pace_mission.gd`. The earlier `training_mission.gd` model is superseded. The final workflow and its unit corrections are recorded in [FINAL-WORKFLOW.md](design-specs/FINAL-WORKFLOW.md); the four-group animation handoff is [VIDEO-EQUATIONS.md](VIDEO-EQUATIONS.md).

### Four connected relationships

1. **Pace and effort produce cleaning work.** Let a(t) = 1 only during active cleaning, otherwise 0. Fatigue starts at F = 0 and is bounded to 0 ≤ F ≤ 0.9. During active cleaning, `dF/dt = 0.08 max(u − 1, 0) − 0.12 max(1 − u, 0)`. Effective cleaning rate is `q = 0.125 × a × u × (1 − F)` sections/min. `W = min(4, ∫q dt)` and clean fraction `C = W/4`. Pace above 1 builds fatigue; below 1 recovers it; exactly 1 holds existing fatigue. This is authored game behavior, not real astronaut performance data. At four complete sections, work and fatigue freeze; energy and dose continue until the deadline.
2. **Cleaning changes solar power.** Dust task index `d = 1 − C`; transmission `T_panel = 0.25 + 0.75C`; `P = 8 × T_panel = 2 + 1.5W` kW. Dust is a task-progress indicator, not measured thickness or a validated optical law. “100% remaining dust” does not mean zero transmitted sunlight. NASA dust research supports the mechanism, not these coefficients.
3. **Power minus load accumulates actual stored energy.** `dE/dt = (P − 1)/60` kWh per simulated minute, with E initially 0.5 and bounded to 0…6 kWh. Power history must be integrated across pace/fatigue changes. The default panel always exceeds the load, so the pre-warning battery charges. The old projected-at-warning 2 kWh reserve goal is removed; final energy is the actual accumulated value.
4. **The authored radiation curve accumulates dose.** `r(t) = 210 × [1 + 3t/30]` µGy/day. `D(t) = ∫r(t)dt/1440 = (210/1440)[t + 3t²/(2 × 30)]` µGy, where t is simulated minutes. The 1440-minute denominator is a 24-hour Earth day, not a sol. Only the baseline is the historical observation. The ramp, fourfold endpoint, and exact warning are fictional; live SDO imagery does not provide them.

Both the fatigue/work clock and radiation clock use simulated minutes. User pauses do not create outside time or work. Comfortable play speed changes real duration, not the equations at a given mission time. The return is automatic; the old early-recall trade-off no longer exists.

### Outcomes and one exact worked example

At 30 simulated minutes, automatically enter shelter and report **WIN if C ≥ 0.8; PASS if 0.4 ≤ C < 0.8; otherwise LOSE**. These are cleaned-fraction game goals, not engineering or medical safety thresholds. Energy and dose explain the run but are not additional hidden win conditions.

Normal pace u = 1 throughout keeps F = 0. After 30 minutes: W = 3.75 sections, C = 93.75%, dust = 6.25%, final P = 7.625 kW, and actual E = **2.40625 kWh**. The energy uses the whole rising power history: `0.5 + [(2 + 7.625)/2 − 1] × (30/60)`, not final power multiplied by the whole round. This example is a WIN; it must not replace another player's actual result.

For every completed round, the same outside duration and curve produce **D(30) = 10.9375 µGy**. Changing cleaning pace cannot change dose in this final design, and lower-dose bonus stars are therefore removed. At full cleaning, waiting still adds energy and dose until automatic entry. Ending the outside-dose activity indoors does not mean radiation indoors is zero.

### Numerical integration

For a constant pace on an interval without a fatigue/work boundary, F changes linearly, q is linear, W and P are quadratic, and E is cubic in time. The engine must integrate those terms and split intervals at fatigue limits, task completion, storage limits, and the deadline. The radiation integral is quadratic in elapsed time. A simple trapezoid of start/end power is **not exact** for this new quadratic-power model; that property belonged to the earlier recall model. Time-step consistency tests must verify the new integration.

## What this version deliberately does not calculate

The live SDO browse image does not supply numerical Mars telemetry. There is no real-time Mars storm prediction, personal dosimeter, radiation-transport solver, pressure-to-radiation regression, medical model, calibrated fatigue/fitness model, detailed electrochemical battery model, oxygen model, or life-support certification in this introductory mission. Historical NASA-supported measurements and links are evidence for the concepts; game parameters supply the short scenario.

Real absorbed dose and dose equivalent depend on particle type, energy spectrum, shielding composition and geometry, secondary particles, location, atmospheric column, and solar conditions. This mission integrates power minus load into stored energy; real electrical storage also requires efficiency, losses, charging limits, temperature, and hardware validation. A calm window scene and an animated astronaut do not establish physically validated terrain, suit motion, or weather.

Heart rate, step count, and ordinary Apple Watch health readings cannot determine absorbed space-radiation dose or sieverts. This release does not collect such data. “Gray strength” is replaced with **absorbed dose**; gray is a unit, not a force or strength.

## Source access and reproducibility

All eight optional concept cards include an HTTPS source link, a real-world fact with context, and a separate scenario assumption. Sources open only on an explicit click; the mission pauses while a card is read. Facts remain available offline in the installed catalog. Opening external evidence needs connectivity.

Reviewed source pages returned HTTP 200 during this audit. The canonical DOI may be access-restricted at the publisher; the Caltech author manuscript supplies the verified Table 1. Links may change later, so source labels and historical context remain in the local catalog. No copyrighted research paper is bundled here. User-supplied design references are archived with byte counts and SHA-256 hashes in [the provenance manifest](design-specs/source/journey-2026-11-14/provenance.json).
