# Red Veil submission workspace

## Working pitch

**You are the astronaut. Your crewmate is counting on you. The sky is disappearing.**

Red Veil is a first-person Mars mission for ages 7–12. Players walk through an outpost, inspect a solar array, clear a dusty visor, manage two energy stores, and reach a shielded refuge. Dust and a solar particle event create different problems. The challenge is to recognize which action helps, when it helps, and what it costs.

Our central learning moment is that a clean panel can still produce almost no power under a dusty sky. The player experiences that distinction through equipment, environmental change, and a companion's radio guidance. A debrief compares the run with a declared fixed strategy under identical modeled weather.

## Ninety-second demonstration

1. **0–15 s:** Show the first-person view and Mira. State the audience and the learning problem.
2. **15–35 s:** Walk to the array. Hold the inspection control. Show visor cleaning consuming suit energy while habitat cleaning uses a different battery.
3. **35–55 s:** Jump to a prepared recording of the darker sky. Explain why surface cleaning cannot clear atmospheric dust. Show conservation preserving essential loads.
4. **55–70 s:** Walk into the refuge. Explain passive installed shielding and the independent particle event.
5. **70–90 s:** Show the debrief and a source/assumption distinction. Close with the next validation step: supervised learner playtesting and scientific review.

A recording must be labeled if time is compressed or scenes are edited. Do not present a scripted demo as a completed user study.
