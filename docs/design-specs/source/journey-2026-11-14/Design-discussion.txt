Here is how KŌMAKU: Shield Protocol works, in plain words.

The situation. Ren is an astronaut standing outside on Mars, near the solar panels. The shelter hatch is 240 m away. Through the window of your control panel you watch her. Your job is to get her inside before a radiation burst arrives. There is no airlock. She is either outside or in the shelter.

Radiation is about time. Radiation adds up like water filling a bucket. Every minute Ren spends outside adds to the bucket. When the burst starts, the bucket fills about 60 times faster outside than before it. Inside the shelter it fills five times slower. So the question is how long it takes her to walk in. Time = distance ÷ speed, so 240 m ÷ 0.8 m/s = 300 s, or 5 minutes. If the burst starts at minute 12 and you want a 2-minute cushion, she must arrive by minute 10. That means she must leave by minute 5. You can do that sum yourself.

Dust is also about time. Dust keeps settling on the solar panels, like snow on a car windshield. Real Mars measurements show about 0.28% more of the panel covered each Mars day when the sky is calm, and a dust storm makes that faster. Less sunlight reaches the cells, so they make less electricity. The panels start already half covered. Ren can clean them, which takes 3 minutes outside, and that brings them to 90% working.

How the two connect: power. Your battery runs the shelter’s air and heat. Dusty panels make little electricity, so the battery drains. Clean panels make more, so it lasts longer.

Your three choices.

Clean the panels. This gives you more power for the days ahead, but Ren stays outside for 3 more minutes.
Conserve power. This switches off optional equipment, so the battery drains more slowly.
Return to the shelter. This starts the walk, and you can’t undo it.
The tension. Minutes outside cost radiation. Cleaning buys power for days. You win if she is inside in time, her total radiation stays under the game’s target, and the battery still has energy after the storm days.

The learning. The panel shows the formulas with your numbers filled in. You will see that one minute outside costs a known amount of radiation, and that cleaning is worth a known amount of battery. I’ll make those two numbers visible side by side so you can weigh them.

does radiation play into the power part coz sun is radiation?

Yes, in a sense, but the two are different kinds of radiation, and the game keeps them apart on purpose.

What the panels use. Sunlight is electromagnetic radiation, made of photons. Panels turn that light into electricity. Dust, whether in the air or on the panels, blocks photons, which is why the sky factor and panel factor shrink the supply.

What hurts Ren. The radiation hazard is ionizing particle radiation. It is made of fast-moving charged particles, such as protons from a solar flare or cosmic rays arriving from deep space. These particles damage cells and electronics, and they pass through dust almost untouched. The Mars measurements behind the game’s background dose (Curiosity’s 210 µGy/day) are of this particle radiation.

Where they meet. Both come from the Sun, and a flare can send a burst of particles at the same moment it sends light. So the burst and the sunlight share a source. They don’t share an effect in the game:

A particle burst does not reduce the panels’ output. Over a few minutes, particle damage to panels is negligible, so radiation correctly has no direct effect on power in the model.
A dust storm cuts sunlight heavily but does almost nothing to particles, and Mars’s thin air gives little shielding either. A dark, stormy sky does not make Ren safer from the burst.
That last point makes a good teaching card, which I’d call “Same Sun, two products”:

Light makes power.
Particles make dose.
Dust blocks one and not the other.
It also explains why the game’s two hazards connect only through minutes outside and not through any physical cause.

If you want radiation to affect power, I could add long-term panel damage from accumulated dose. It would be real physics, but too slow to matter in this game, so I’d keep it out unless you want it as a later extension.

Clean before the burst: It costs no extra radiation. Radiation at this point is the same small background everywhere, inside or out.
Clean after the burst has started: The same 3 minutes outside adds 24 µGy of radiation that Ren would not have taken inside the shelter.

Plan	Power lasts	Verdict

Cleaned panels, no Conserve	5.4 sols	Too short

1. Radiation is a clock.

The panels make some power. The shelter uses some power.
Panels make 1.44, shelter uses 0.70, so the gap is 1.44 − 0.70 = 0.74.
Dust shaves a little off the panels every day, about 0.045 a day.
Days until the gap hits zero = gap ÷ daily loss = 0.74 ÷ 0.045 ≈ 16 days.
The player learns that power is supply minus demand, and that a slow, steady loss adds up to a deadline.
3. The two meet in one choice.

Cleaning takes 3 minutes outside and adds about 14 days of power.

The player learns that every choice has a price in two currencies, minutes and days, and that the price can change with timing.
What the player takes away overall:

How to work backward from a deadline.
That power is a gap that shrinks, not a pile.
That dust in the air and dust on the panels are different, and only one can be wiped off.

what is gy and please don't use shortcut or unclear words

Gy is short for gray. It is the unit scientists use to count how much radiation energy something has absorbed.

What it measures. Radiation carries energy. When it passes through a body, some of that energy stays behind. One gray means one joule of energy was absorbed by each kilogram of material. A joule is a small unit of energy. You use roughly one joule to lift a small apple about one meter.

What a microgray is. The symbol µGy stands for microgray. It is one millionth of a gray. Microgray is the right size for this game, because everyday amounts of radiation are tiny numbers when counted in whole grays.

How the game uses it.

On Mars, the natural background radiation measured by NASA’s Curiosity rover is about 210 microgray per day. That works out to roughly 9 microgray per hour. The game rounds this to 10.
Ren’s total in the winning plan is 41 microgray. The game’s limit is 60 microgray.
Two things it is not.

It is not a measure of health risk. The gray counts energy absorbed, and it does not say how harmful that energy is to a person. Doctors use a different unit for harm, called the sievert. The game deliberately avoids showing sieverts.
The 60 microgray limit is invented for the game. It is not a real safety limit for astronauts.
Easy way to picture it. Imagine rain falling on a bucket. The microgray count is how much water the bucket has collected so far. The rain rate, in microgray per hour, is how fast the bucket fills. During the burst the rain gets much heavier outside than inside the shelter, which is why Ren should be indoors before it starts.

I built a small simulation engine where radiation, time, speed and dust all connect through one number: how long Ren stays outside. The relationship image and the engine file are attached.

The equation chain (the same five lines are in the engine file):

Walk time: t = d / v (distance divided by speed)
Sections cleaned: n = floor((W − t) / τ) (warning time minus walk time, divided by the time per section)
Time outside: T = t + n·τ
Days of power: P = (S₀ + g·n) / k (dust loss rate k = 0.28 per sol)
Radiation: D = r₁·T, rising to r₂ after the warning ends
Real data behind it

Dust: panels lose about 0.28 points of power per sol (Pathfinder).
Radiation and air pressure: radiation rises and falls with air pressure, with a correlation of 0.96, a swing of about 5% over a day (Curiosity study).
Dust and power: Opportunity’s power tracked the dust settled on its panels strongly (0.84), and suspended dust in the air much less (NASA analysis).
Speed: suited walking speeds are expected below 1 m/s (spacesuit study), so the game’s 0.8 m/s is realistic.
What the correlation map shows. These numbers come from 20,000 simulated runs of the engine, not from measured data. The real data set the constants and ranges.

Distance drives walk time most strongly (+0.86).
Warning time drives radiation dose most strongly (+0.85). A longer warning lets a player do more work, so they stay outside longer. That was not what I expected.
Air pressure barely matters (+0.10), so a realistic effect can be tiny.
Speed works in cliffs (panel B). Slowing down a little can lose a whole cleaning section, which is a worse drop than the smooth line players might expect.
Two exchange rates the player can learn

One cleaned section buys 7.1 days of power and costs 0.18 mSv of radiation.
Ren’s safe working radius is d_max = v·(W − n·τ), which is 864 m for 2 sections and 576 m for 3 sections at 0.8 m/s.

what's the formula between all factors

All the factors connect in one chain, and time outside is the link.

Step by step

Walk time: t = d ÷ v
Sections cleaned: n = floor((W − t) ÷ τ)
Time outside: T = t + n × τ
Days of power: P = (S₀ + g × n) ÷ k
Radiation: D = p × r₁ × T (with r₂ replacing r₁ for any time outside after the warning ends)
All in one formula (for a player who leaves in time)

P = (S₀ + g × floor((W − d ÷ v) ÷ τ)) ÷ k

That is days of power, with speed, distance, warning time, cleaning pace and dust rate all inside it.

What each letter means

Letter	Meaning	Value	Real or simulated
d	Distance to shelter	432 m	Simulated
v	Walking speed	0.8 m/s	Realistic, since suited astronauts walk below about 1 m/s
W	Warning time	1,800 s (30 min)	Real
τ	Time per cleaning section	360 s	Simulated
S₀	Spare power at the start	1 point	Simulated
g	Power gained per section	2 points	Simulated
k	Power lost to dust	0.28 points per day	Real
r₁, r₂	Radiation per minute outside, before and after the warning ends	0.03 and 0.15 mSv	Simulated
p	Air-pressure effect on radiation	about 1 ± 0.025	Real size
Worked example

Walk time: 432 ÷ 0.8 = 540 s
Sections: floor((1,800 − 540) ÷ 360) = 3
Time outside: 540 + 3 × 360 = 1,620 s, or 27 minutes
Power: (1 + 2 × 3) ÷ 0.28 = 25 days
Radiation: 0.03 × 27 = 0.81 mSv
Win rule: Ren is inside before W ends (T ≤ W) and power is at least 15 days.

Two useful by-products:

Exchange rate: each section buys g ÷ k = 7.1 days of power for 6 more minutes outside.
Safe radius: d_max = v × (W − n × τ), which is 576 m for 3 sections.
