## Summary

On Mars, dust steals the power and radiation limits the time to fix it.

KŌMAKU: Dust & Radiation is a Mars mission-control game for ages 7–12 by Team Roshan Xingbo. We turn complex space science into a story young astronauts can understand. From an outpost window, players guide their teammate’s solar-panel cleaning, watch dust clear and energy collect, and explore radiation exposure during a fictional storm countdown.

We built a short, replayable 3D mission with science explanations and results connected to simple equations. Players practise predicting, monitoring resources, comparing strategies, and explaining cause and effect.

NASA’s Curiosity radiation measurements, Pathfinder dust research, and rover power studies ground the learning. Measured evidence is distinguished from game settings.

Our contribution to the Junior Astronaut Mission Trainer challenge is connecting dust, radiation, and energy in one visible experience. It gives children a practical way to explore scientific decisions, rather than memorize isolated facts. Learning gains still need testing with children and educators.

## What is innovative, and what impact can we defend?

We bring separate bodies of NASA research into one visible learning loop: predict → act → observe → explain → retry. Dust changes modeled solar output; solar generation minus habitat consumption changes stored energy; radiation dose accumulates over time. The child can inspect these relationships instead of memorizing isolated facts.

This is a connection between teaching models, not a newly discovered statistical correlation between dust and radiation. In the current mission, pace changes cleaning and stored energy; completed runs share radiation dose and outside duration. Do not claim the player currently optimizes a changing radiation-risk outcome.

Intended skills: reading rates and units, distinguishing power from energy and dose rate from dose, monitoring resources, comparing strategies, and explaining cause and effect. Accessible offline practice and inspectable sources are present benefits. Improved knowledge, long-term retention, and real astronaut competence have not been demonstrated. Evaluate these next through learner predictions, explanation questions and later recall.
