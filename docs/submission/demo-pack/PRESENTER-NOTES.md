# KŌMAKU presentation notes

## 60-second pitch

On Mars, dust steals power, and radiation limits the time to fix it. We’re Team Roshan Xingbo, building KŌMAKU: Dust & Radiation for young explorers.

From mission control, players guide solar-panel cleaning, watch energy change, and discover why their choices matter. NASA’s Curiosity radiation measurements and Pathfinder dust research ground the lesson. Simple equations connect actions to outcomes.

Our live Cloudflare lab brings NASA observations to the screen. OpenSpace adds a Mars scale reference; TimescaleDB records observation checks. A built-in guide keeps explanations available while our Gemini project’s free-tier access is blocked.

The goal is scientific reasoning: predict, monitor, compare, and explain. Next, we’ll test learning with children and educators. Tomorrow, that child could be making these calls on Mars.

## Slide 1

On Mars, dust steals power, and radiation limits the time to fix it. For young learners, the challenge is understanding how invisible quantities connect. We turn that problem into a mission they can see and influence. This is an educational simulation, not astronaut certification.

## Slide 2

From mission control, players guide Ren’s panel-cleaning pace. They see dust clear, energy change, and the mission end. The short round supports prediction, observation, explanation, and another attempt. The current pace choice changes cleaning and energy; completed rounds share outside exposure time and dose.

## Slide 3

NASA’s Curiosity radiation measurements, Pathfinder dust experiment, and rover-power research ground the lesson. Dust affects solar output. Generation minus consumption changes stored energy. Dose accumulates over time. Our contribution is connecting these relationships through feedback, not discovering a new physical law. The game’s storm and equipment values are teaching assumptions.

## Slide 4

The companion lab is live on Cloudflare and retrieves NASA’s latest available solar image. OpenSpace provides an attributed Mars scale reference. We used self-hosted TimescaleDB’s Apache-licensed core to record actual retrieval checks; its displayed audit is a snapshot, not a hosted Tiger Data service. Gemini integration is implemented, but this key’s project rejects free-tier generation. The working fallback is explicitly labelled Built-in guide. We do not pretend it is AI output.

## Slide 5

The value is practising a way of thinking: predict, monitor, compare, and explain. Offline missions and visible equations make that practice inspectable and repeatable. We have not yet measured learning gains. Next we want to test understanding with children and educators, then extend the experience to different crews and constraints. Tomorrow, that child could be the one making these calls on Mars.
